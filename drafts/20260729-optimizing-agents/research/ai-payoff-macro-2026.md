# Does AI pay off outside tech? Aggregate evidence, 2026

Research notes for "How to Customize Agents, and When to Own Them". Collected 2026-10-25.

Purpose: the case studies in workflow-redesign-2026.md show gains at single firms. This file tracks the economy-wide view, which so far shows little payoff outside the companies that sell AI. Relevant to "Turning adoption into business gains".

## Apollo, "No Signs of AI Payoff Outside the Tech Sector" (Oct 2026)

- **Source:** Torsten Slok (Apollo Chief Economist), The Daily Spark, October 24, 2026. https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector
- **Status:** text, chart titles and source notes checked by me. Chart values are read off the chart.
- **Claim:** "Despite massive investments and widespread hype around artificial intelligence, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech."
- **Bottom line (quote):** "the AI story will be decided in the S&P 493, not the Mag 7, and so far, there is no evidence that revenue growth or margins are improving outside the tech sector."

## My read: how much weight it bears

- **Charts 1–2 mostly show who sells AI, not who gains from using it.** The Mag 7 include Nvidia and the big cloud providers. Everyone else's AI spend is their revenue, so a rising Mag 7 alongside a flat S&P 493 is partly one flow of money seen from both sides. Mag 7 layoffs since 2022 also raise revenue per worker.
- **"Zero" is stronger than the evidence.** An index average over 493 firms has no counterfactual. Margins over this period also moved with rates, tariffs and wages. Gains at a minority of firms would be diluted in the average.
- **The productivity chart is the weakest leg.** Year-over-year TFP is noisy. The "productivity J-curve" argument predicts measured productivity dips while firms invest in reorganizing, before any payoff shows. Computers also showed up "everywhere but in the productivity statistics" for years before the late-1990s jump, which is visible on the same chart.
- **Ramp's sample isn't the S&P 493.** Ramp's customers lean toward startups and mid-market companies. Large enterprises often buy AI through cloud contracts and enterprise agreements, so Ramp probably undercounts their spending.
- **Concentration is normal for spend data.** In the same table, non-AI SaaS (91.8%) and paid ads (91.9%) are almost as concentrated. AI is more lopsided than usual, but only somewhat.
- **The "pulling back" and "unwilling to pay" claims are asserted, not charted.** Nothing shown measures a pullback over time.
- **Fair version:** the aggregate data doesn't yet show AI paying off outside the sellers, and heavy adoption is concentrated in VC-backed firms. That supports "not yet," not "never."

## Fit with the article

- Consistent with the article's point that adoption alone doesn't move the business; gains depend on redesigning the work (workflow-redesign-2026.md, cases A, B and G).
- "Does not need the latest model" fits the cost advice: route steps to cheaper models before paying for the frontier.
