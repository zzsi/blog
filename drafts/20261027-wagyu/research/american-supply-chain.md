# American wagyu supply chain: research notes

Collected 2026-10-27 by a research agent. Web search was unavailable, so all sources were fetched from known URLs or from links found on fetched pages. Everything below was confirmed by fetch unless marked otherwise. Company pages are self-reports.

## 1. Genetics

- **First imports, 1975 or 1976.**
  - AWA FAQ: "Wagyu cattle were first imported into the United States in 1975 when two black and two red bulls were imported by Morris Whitney." https://wagyu.org/company/faqs
  - The AWA museum page lists each bull (Judo, Mt. Fuji, Mazda, Rueshaw) as "Imported in 1976 by Morris Whitney". https://wagyu.org/company/awhf/awhf-historic-museum
  - Texas Monthly (2023) also says 1976. https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets.wagyu.org/How-Wagyu-Beef-Came-to-the-U.S.-and-Texas-–-Texas-Monthly.pdf
  - Write "1975–76" or "the mid-1970s".
- **The 1990s imports.** Texas Monthly: "In July 1993, a Northwest Airlines 747 ... loaded with three Wagyu cows and two Wagyu bulls ... In total, 180 live Wagyu were shipped to the U.S. in that time ... Shipments ceased once again in 1997 when the Japanese outlawed the exporting of Wagyu cattle." (Japan never legally outlawed it; see history.md §5.)
- **The founders.** The AWA museum page, quoting Wagyu International: "A total of 167 Black Wagyu is known to have been exported ... 16 Red Wagyu ... A total of 221 Wagyu cattle brought genetics ... the basis of all Wagyu and Akaushi in USA, Canada and Australia."
- **A small gene pool.**
  - WSU, Scraggs et al. 2014: "A total of 4132 full-blood Wagyu cattle pedigrees ... effective population size averaged 17 between the years 1994 and 2011." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24373025/
  - Early breeder: "We started line breeding and soon ran into problems with inbreeding." https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets.wagyu.org/Wagyu-History-in-the-United-States.pdf
- **AWA definitions.** From the AWA Rulebook, revised 07-21-2026: https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets.wagyu.org/AWA-Rulebook-Revised-07-21-2026.pdf
  - **Fullblood:** "animals whose sire and dam are registered as 100% Fullblood Wagyu ... All parentage must be DNA verified."
  - **Purebred:** "minimum percentage of Wagyu blood of at least 93.75% (15/16 and above)."
  - **Percentage:** "at least one parent (sire or dam) registered as a Fullblood or Purebred."
  - **Content Registry:** "a minimum of 50% Wagyu genetics" (for animals of unknown parentage).

## 2. The typical animal

- **Crosses (self-reports).**
  - SRF: "American Wagyu is a hybrid cross of pure Japanese Wagyu and premium American cattle." https://www.snakeriverfarms.com/about-us
  - SRF 2026: "the cross between Japanese Wagyu and American Angus that defines SRF's beef." https://www.snakeriverfarms.com/blogs/srf-journal/where-does-snake-river-farms-beef-come-from-meet-the-ranch-behind-every-cut
  - Mishima: "100% Kuroge Washu bulls bred with American cows." https://www.mishimareserve.com/mishima-reserve-method
- **Why Angus.** Australian Wagyu Association: "Angus is the most common cross with Wagyu ... Crossbreeding enables production of large numbers of Wagyu-content animals within a single generation." https://www.wagyu.org.au/for-members/fullblood-and-commercial-breeding
- **The trade-off.** Jaborek et al. 2023: "Wagyu-sired cattle selected for marbling potential had a lesser ADG, DMI, more marbling ... than Angus-sired cattle." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36592748/
- **Share that is crossbred.** No figure found. Texas Monthly: "Most American Wagyu beef cattle are crossbreeds."
- **A fullblood exception.** Lone Mountain (self-report): "our cattle are never crossbred. They are 100% Wagyu." https://lonemountainwagyu.com/pages/about-us

## 3. Production stages

- **Commodity baseline.** USDA ERS: "The length of time a calf spends in a feedlot can range from 90 to 300 days." Feedlots are "concentrated in the Great Plains region but are also located in parts of the Corn Belt, Southwest, and Pacific Northwest." https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/animal-products/cattle-beef/sector-at-a-glance
- **Wagyu time on feed.**
  - Agri Beef: "on feed an average of three times longer than traditional U.S. commodity beef animals." https://www.agribeef.com/exceptional-beef/snake-river-farms/
  - Agri Beef's own commodity cattle: "roughly 140-day[s]"; larger cattle, "roughly 200 days". https://www.agribeef.com/real-families/cattle-feeding/
  - Lone Mountain: "over 500-600 days."
  - Mishima: "25 to 28 months (18 to 20 is an industry standard)." https://www.mishimareserve.com/about
  - Research: a "552-d feeding period" for Wagyu-cross steers (Lunt et al. 1993, cited in Jaborek 2023). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9831106/
- **Feeding and processing.**
  - Agri Beef feed yards: "American Falls, ID ... Parma, ID ... Moses Lake, WA."
  - "Washington Beef is Agri Beef's state-of-the-art processing plant located in Toppenish, WA." https://www.agribeef.com/real-families/processing-wa-beef/
  - SRF: "We process every animal at our state-of-the-art facilities in Washington and Idaho."
- **Seedstock.** SRF: "we have more genetics on file than any other producer and maintain a bull development center." https://www.snakeriverfarms.com/pages/what-is-american-wagyu-beef
- **Distribution.** Agri Beef sells to "retailers and restaurants" and exports to "more than 30 countries." https://www.agribeef.com/ SRF sells direct to consumers.
- **Texas concentration.** Unconfirmed.

## 4. Labeling and grading

- **FSIS breed claims.** "Wagyu (American Kobe)" is a listed breed claim. The required documentation is a "genotype (traceable to one registered parent or two registered grandparents with a breed association)". No minimum Wagyu percentage is stated.
  - Source: FSIS Labeling Guideline, Dec 2019, read via a Wayback copy (fsis.usda.gov returned 403). https://www.fsis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media_file/2021-02/RaisingClaims.pdf
  - A 2024 revision is unconfirmed.
- **The 46.875% figure is press arithmetic.** Texas Monthly says beef labeled Wagyu "could be anywhere from 46.875 percent to 100 percent Wagyu". That is its own math, not USDA text.
- **Prime.** USDA AMS: Prime requires "a minimum slightly abundant amount of marbling." https://www.ams.usda.gov/grades-standards/carcass-beef-grades-and-standards
  - Numeric scale: "700-799 = slightly abundant" (Jaborek 2023).
  - USDA has no grade above Prime.
- **USDA AMS G-162, "Authentic Wagyu Beef Program", dated January 24, 2025.** Requires "U.S. Prime and have a minimum marbling score of Moderately Abundant00 or higher." https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/G162AuthenticWagyuBeefProgram.pdf
- **AWA "Authentic Wagyu" PVP.** https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets.wagyu.org/Manual-RQMT-001-002.pdf
  - Claims: "Born, raised and harvested in USA"; "Breed Verification via American Wagyu Association Registration and DNA Parent Verification"; marbling "800/Moderately Abundant00 or higher".
  - The manual header says "Issue Date: 01/25/27", likely a typo.
  - The AMS Official Listing of Approved PVPs fetched today shows no Wagyu or AWA entry.
- **"Wagyu Excellence" was not found.**

## 5. Fraud and DNA verification

- **Restaurant mislabeling investigations.** Unconfirmed: Forbes (2012) and Texas Monthly returned 403.
- **The AWA's aim.** The Authentic Wagyu programs aim to "protect the Wagyu name." https://wagyu.org/for-producers/authentic-wagyu-pvp
- **PVP DNA checks.** "random DNA TraceBack sampling. Random Samples return results consistent to sampled meat products to actual animal DNA."

## 6. Scale and 2025–26 developments

- **AWA membership.** "approximately 1700" members. https://wagyu.org/company/americanwagyuassocaition-about
- **US wagyu head count (secondhand).** Texas Monthly 2023: "the American Wagyu Association estimates there are around 40,000 cattle in the U.S. that are anywhere from half to full-blood Wagyu". No AWA primary source found.
- **Largest producer.** SRF claims to be the "#1 Largest producer of American Wagyu" (self-report).
- **US herd.** NASS: "All cattle and calves ... as of January 1, 2026 totaled 86.2 million head." https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/catl0126.pdf
- **Cattle prices.** ERS (10/19/2026): "Cattle prices for 2026 are adjusted to reflect recent price strength." https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/animal-products/cattle-beef/market-outlook
- **New packer partner.** May 13, 2026: Real Kansas Meats became "one of the first packing facilities to become a licensed Authentic Wagyu partner." https://s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/assets.wagyu.org/Real-Kansas-Meats-will-be-a-licensed-Packer-Partner-for-the-Authentic-Wagyu-Certified-Beef-Program.pdf
- **Imperial American Wagyu and JBS.** The Imperial "Our Legacy" page names JBS. https://imperialwagyubeef.com/legacy/

## 7. Imports into the US

- **Quotas and tariffs.** HTSUS 2026 Rev. 21, Ch. 2. https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/file?release=currentRelease&filename=Chapter%202
  - No Japan-specific beef quota. Japan falls under "Other countries or areas 52,005" metric tons.
  - In-quota rate: "4.4¢/kg"; over-quota rate: "26.4%".
  - Australia's quota: "378,214" metric tons.
  - A 2025–26 reciprocal-tariff layer is unconfirmed.
- **Australian wagyu exports.** The Australian Wagyu Association gives both "more than 90%" and "Approximately 80%" exported, on two pages. https://www.wagyu.org.au/worlds-luxury-beef ; https://www.wagyu.org.au/worlds-luxury-beef/why-breed-wagyu
